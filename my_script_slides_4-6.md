## Slide 4 · Screening (30 s)
Not every product does. If China is three hundred percent cheaper on headphones, no tariff changes the answer.
So we ran one rule on both datasets. Step one: keep categories where the two cheapest origins are within twenty percent on unit cost. Step two: keep the ones where three or more countries sit that close.
Consumer goods: thirty-six pulled, twenty-three comparable, seven near-tied, four multi-country. Industrial goods: thirteen curated, seven near-tied, five multi-country.
Why closeness? Because the break-even tariff is just the cost gap: a four-percent gap flips on a four-point tariff move.
（重點只有一句：損益兩平 = 成本差距。數字念慢一點，手指著漏斗）

## Slide 5 · Nine categories (12 s)
That leaves nine categories in five industries, from apparel and footwear to auto parts and electronics. In all of them the cheapest countries are within twelve percent on cost, and for the industrial ones the real duties paid in 2025 show the tariff is the whole decision.
These importers are our first customers.
（手掃九張卡，不唸細節；被問到再講煞車油管的例子：中國付 50%、墨西哥付 9%，2% 的價差變成 40% 的落地成本差）

## If you get a question
- Why 20%? “It is the range a realistic tariff or currency move can cover within a year.”
- Why 3% share cutoff? “Below that an origin usually cannot supply a real order; it is a scale filter.”
- Aren’t unit values just averages? “Yes, they find the market. The decision uses the importer’s own quotes.”
