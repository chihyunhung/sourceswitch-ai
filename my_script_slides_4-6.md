## Slide 4 · Screening (35 s)
Not every product does. If China is three hundred percent cheaper on headphones, no tariff changes the answer.
So we ran one rule on both datasets. Step one: keep categories where the two cheapest origins are within twenty percent on unit cost. Step two: keep the ones where three or more countries sit that close.
Consumer goods: thirty-six pulled, twenty-three comparable, seven near-tied, four multi-country. Industrial goods: thirteen curated, seven near-tied, five multi-country.
Why closeness? Because the break-even tariff is simply the cost gap. A four percent gap flips on a four-point tariff move. A three-hundred percent gap never does.
（重點只有一句：損益兩平 = 成本差距。數字念慢一點，手指著漏斗）

## Slide 5 · Nine categories (25 s)
That leaves nine categories in five industries.
On the consumer side: knit trousers, leather footwear, T-shirts, wooden furniture, where China, Indonesia, Bangladesh or Canada are within one to eleven percent of each other.
On the industrial side: brake hoses, CNC controls, heater parts, bumpers and suspension parts. Here we also know the duty each country actually paid in 2025, and China paid thirty to fifty points more than the cheapest rival on a cost gap of under twelve percent.
These importers are a few percent away from a different answer. They are our first customers.
（藍色四張唸類別就好，橘色五張強調「實繳關稅差 30–50 點」）

## If you get a question
- Why 20%? “It is the range a realistic tariff or currency move can cover within a year.”
- Why 3% share cutoff? “Below that an origin usually cannot supply a real order; it is a scale filter.”
- Aren’t unit values just averages? “Yes, they find the market. The decision uses the importer’s own quotes.”
